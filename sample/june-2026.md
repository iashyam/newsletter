I have been a minimalist for very long, as long as I can remember. I own as many clothes as I can pack and leave. I have one pair of shoes that you will see me wearing at every occasion. I have got as many friends as I see on a daily basis. Other than the books I have read, I have no interest in collecting anything. I hardly buy anything that I don't use on a daily basis. 

But lately, I have been steering away from this trait. I have bought a bedside table, on which I have put an alarm clock, a jug with a glass, a lamp, and a book. I have bought skincare products such as sunscreen and moisturiser. I own a monitor screen, an external keyboard and mouse, and an external speaker. I would have considered all these things unnecessary and a total waste of money; but now I am investing in them. 

The idea is that these things should represent me and my personality. The things that I own, the things that I collect, give the details of my life around the basic prototype of a person that exists in the world. When I look at the books I have read and collected, I see something that makes me different from anyone else, and when I see the same books in someone else's house, I feel a kind of connection. And if you strip that detail, I am just another human being. 

I came across this beautiful video randomly on YouTube recommendation titled [When did the world get so ugly?](https://youtu.be/tWYxrowovts?si=K-_Z4uIubB7D1urD). And what I felt was that when we migrated to minimalism and flat design, we stripped off the details that represented each thing and made them unique. And now we are left with a world where everything looks the same. Every building, every logo, every icon looks the same. And when we look around, every boy around me is following me the same actor as inspiration, and everyone looks the same. We all watch the same shows, the same songs, and our uniqueness is being left behind. And I think that somehow or the other, our minimalistic philosophy is behind this.
