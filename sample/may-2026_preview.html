The Matrix Is Real

One of the biggest problems faced by machine learning engineers was to convert language into numbers. The subtle thing about language is that it is arbitrary. The sounds that we make to communicate have little to do with their meaning. And that means language can't be programmatically scripted.

One of the best attempts to represent language into numbers was the paper Word2Vec. They found a way to represent a word in a vocabulary as a high-dimensional vector which will represent its meaning. The trick was to make the AI predict the words within a sentence. For example, trying to predict ‘The monkey ——- a mango’. The word in the blank will depend on the context around the word. And the scientists nailed this.

Since the vectors were prepared according to the context, they represented the semantic meaning of the word rather than just the text. For example, in this system, the vector for damsel and princess will lie close together. The fact that I find most amazing is the following: if you subtract the vector for men from the vector for king, and then add it to the vector for woman, you will get the vector for queen.

$$V(\text{king}) - V(\text{man}) + V(\text{woman}) = V(\text{queen})$$
The idea of having semantic meaning as vectors is much more powerful than it seems. It can be extended into the hypothesis that anything can be represented as vectors, including ideologies, philosophies, and scientific theories. The social media platforms can collect your data along different axes and put them in a vector space. YOU, including all your ideologies, your personalities, can be represented as a vector. And what is used to manipulate vectors: Matrices.

Matrices are all around us. ChatGPT, for example, is a collection of matrices. Your email
classifying emails into spam and not spam is a matrix. Your digital life is more or less governed by matrices.

The most obvious way the matrices manipulate our lives is through recommendation systems. What perception you have of the world is dependent on what information you get about it. That information largely comes from news articles, YouTube videos, and tweets. And what you get recommended is decided by a collection of matrices. Under the hood, all the recommendation systems are just matrices.

So Matrix is indeed real and indeed it's affecting our lives in a matter way more direct than the other.
